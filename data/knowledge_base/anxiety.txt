ANXIETY

Anxiety is a common emotional response to situations that feel uncertain, stressful, or threatening. It can involve thoughts, emotions, physical sensations, and changes in behavior.

Common experiences associated with anxiety may include excessive worry, feeling restless or tense, difficulty concentrating, irritability, and changes in sleep. Physical experiences can include a faster heartbeat, sweating, trembling, or stomach discomfort.

Anxiety can occur in everyday situations, such as before an examination, interview, presentation, or important decision. These temporary feelings do not necessarily indicate an anxiety disorder.

Anxiety becomes more concerning when fear or worry is persistent, difficult to control, or significantly interferes with everyday activities, relationships, school, work, or other areas of life. Different anxiety disorders can have different patterns of symptoms.

Factors associated with anxiety can include stressful life experiences, ongoing pressure, sleep difficulties, individual temperament, and biological or environmental influences. Anxiety can also occur alongside other mental health difficulties.

Helpful general approaches for managing everyday stress and anxiety can include maintaining regular sleep, physical activity, balanced routines, relaxation practices, social support, and talking with a trusted person. When anxiety is persistent or interferes substantially with daily life, speaking with a qualified mental-health professional can be appropriate.
