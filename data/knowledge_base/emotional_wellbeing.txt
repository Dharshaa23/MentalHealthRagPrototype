EMOTIONAL WELLBEING

Emotional wellbeing refers broadly to how people experience, understand, and manage their emotions and how these experiences relate to their overall functioning and quality of life.

Emotional wellbeing does not mean feeling positive emotions all the time. A person can experience difficult or negative emotions while still having overall emotional wellbeing. The ability to recognize emotions, respond to them appropriately, and maintain meaningful relationships can contribute to wellbeing.

Emotions can influence attention, decision-making, relationships, motivation, and perceptions of everyday experiences. Patterns of emotional experience over time may therefore be relevant to overall wellbeing.

Several factors can contribute to emotional wellbeing, including social relationships, coping abilities, life circumstances, physical health, sleep, and opportunities for meaningful and enjoyable activities.

Emotional wellbeing is related to, but not identical with, other aspects of wellbeing such as psychological, social, and physical wellbeing. These dimensions can influence one another.

Supporting emotional wellbeing can involve developing healthy coping strategies, maintaining supportive relationships, taking care of physical health, engaging in meaningful activities, and seeking appropriate professional support when difficulties persist.

This document is intended for educational purposes and is not a diagnostic or therapeutic resource.
