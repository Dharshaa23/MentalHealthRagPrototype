STRESS

Stress is the physical and emotional response that can occur when a person perceives that demands or challenges exceed, or may exceed, their available resources.

Stress can be triggered by many situations, including academic pressure, work demands, relationship difficulties, major changes, uncertainty, financial concerns, or lack of sleep.

Short-term stress can sometimes help a person respond to a challenge by increasing alertness and motivation. However, prolonged or intense stress can affect concentration, mood, sleep, energy, and general wellbeing.

Common experiences during stressful periods can include feeling overwhelmed, tense, worried, tired, irritable, or unable to concentrate. People can respond to stress differently.

General strategies that may support stress management include organizing tasks into manageable steps, taking appropriate breaks, maintaining regular sleep, engaging in physical activity, using relaxation techniques, and seeking social support.

If stress becomes persistent, overwhelming, or substantially affects everyday functioning, talking with a qualified mental-health professional may be helpful.

This document is intended for educational purposes and does not provide a diagnosis.
